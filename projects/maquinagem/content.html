Maquinagem

2026
Film / Book

Maquinagem ("Machination") observes the organization of urban life based on its rhythms of work, movement, and repetition. The film is constructed in direct contact with a territory marked by continuous production, where the construction site is not only a setting, but a model of operation. The fragmented editing follows the rhythm of the construction process itself: circular gestures, repeated movements, small variations that only reveal transformation after long intervals. Repetition does not act as an expressive resource, but as a method of observation, bringing the gaze closer to the dilated time of manual labor. Between these blocks of labor, images of everyday life emerge—a boy playing soccer, people walking, brief moments of pause. These scenes do not oppose work; they surround and traverse it, revealing how urban life is structured by cycles of effort, waiting, and displacement. The boy on the soccer field appears as a transitional figure, still outside the formal productive circuit, but already inscribed in its gestures. His repeated movements—running, kicking, competing with himself—echo the mechanical effort of the workers and anticipate the future that defines the surroundings. By articulating work, daily life, and memory, Maquinagem proposes a view of the city as a continuously operating system, where wear and tear, permanence, and transformation coexist in the same space.
